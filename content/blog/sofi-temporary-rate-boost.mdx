SoFi consistently shows up near the top of "best high-yield savings" lists, and
on the surface the rate looks fantastic. But the advertised number comes with two
conditions that change the math considerably. If you are picking an account based
on that headline, it is worth understanding exactly what you are signing up for.

## Condition one: the boost is temporary

A large part of SoFi's most attractive rate has historically come from a
**promotional boost** for new money — an extra slice of APY layered on top of the
base rate for a limited window, commonly around six months.

That structure is great for the first half-year and much less great afterward. In
our tracking, the step-down once a boost ends has been on the order of **roughly
0.7 percentage points** — enough to drop the account from "best in class" to
"perfectly ordinary." The headline that drew you in is, in practice, an
introductory rate with an expiration date attached.

## Condition two: you need direct deposit

The other catch is qualification. To earn SoFi's top savings tier, you generally
have to **set up direct deposit** into the account (or meet a recurring-deposit
requirement). Without it, you fall to a lower rate.

Direct deposit is not a casual toggle. It means routing your paycheck — the
single most important automatic transfer in your financial life — through SoFi.
That is precisely the kind of deep hook that makes an account *sticky*: once your
paycheck lands there, the cost of leaving later (even after the boost fades) goes
up sharply. It is the inertia trap, but front-loaded into the sign-up.

## So is it a bad account?

Not necessarily. If you genuinely want to move your direct deposit and bank with
SoFi as your primary hub, the package can make sense, and the first six months
are legitimately strong. The problem is the mismatch between how the rate is
*marketed* (a simple, very high APY) and how it actually *works* (a conditional,
time-limited rate that requires handing over your paycheck).

A few things to weigh before you chase it:

- **Will the rate still be competitive after the boost ends?** Look at the base
  rate, not the promotional one.
- **Do you actually want to move your direct deposit?** If not, you may never
  qualify for the headline number in the first place.
- **What is the real, blended rate over a full year?** Six months of a great rate
  plus six months of an ordinary one averages out to something far less exciting
  than the banner ad.

## The bottom line

SoFi's top rate is best understood as a promotional, conditions-attached offer
rather than a durable APY. Read the boost terms and the direct-deposit
requirement before you decide — and compare its *base* rate against accounts that
simply pay a steady, no-strings rate year-round.
